THE CREW — Game Concept

Working foundation, saved September 14, 2026. This document records the creator's stated ideas. Unspecified mechanics remain open.

## Core concept

THE CREW is a PC game in which the player chooses from a roster of **6,000 characters** to build a **five-person crew, including the player character**: the player plus four other members.

The crew is described as a **global crime interception team**. Clients publish jobs online (in-game), and the player chooses which jobs to accept around the world.

The central challenge is understanding the crew and choosing suitable jobs. Each member's strengths, limitations, history, and role affect the job.

## Character roster and presentation

- There are 6,000 characters to choose from. The number refers to the character roster. (5,000 as first stated; 1,000 specialists added 16 September 2026.)
- Characters are male or female. There is no neutral gender option or pronoun system.
- The overall game should be very basic; the intended level of mechanical complexity has not yet been specified.
- Earlier visual direction: a very simple, clean black THE CREW wordmark on a white background, with black-and-white face avatars and changeable characteristics.

## Five-person crew

Every member has an individual profile, covering:

- Attributes and capabilities: what the character can and cannot do.
- Experience.
- Techniques.
- Knowledge.
- Education.
- Time in jail.
- Time on the streets.
- Commander or soldier qualities or role.

The player needs to understand these profiles when selecting the crew and accepting jobs. All relevant aspects of crew members affect the job; specific calculations and outcome rules are not yet defined.

## Countries, categories, and jobs

Jobs vary by country and reflect local context, including:

- Religion and demographics.
- Geography and topography.
- Currency and economic conditions.
- Politics.
- Language and culture.

Each country offers a selection of job categories. Countries can share categories and jobs, but countries do not all have the same categories or the same jobs.

The player can choose the jobs best suited to the crew. Crew abilities and limitations must matter to that choice.

## Details still open

These are unspecified details, not additional requirements:

- The precise meaning of "crime interception": the crew has not been defined as police, criminals, mercenaries, or another organization.
- Whether commander and soldier are fixed roles, selectable assignments, or descriptive character qualities.
- How missions are played, how results are calculated, and what "very basic" means for the interface and mechanics.
- The game engine, country list, job categories, progression, recruitment rules, and attribute values.

## Decisions since this document

The text above is kept as written. Decisions that close one of its open details are logged here, dated.

- **15 September 2026 — The crew are criminals, not police.** Closes the first item under "Details still open". The crew is a criminal crew for hire; the clients online are the buyers. The game's copy, the README and the *notoriety* measure follow from it.
- **15 September 2026 — The player is always the commander, for now.** Closes the second item under "Details still open" for the single-player game. Commander/soldier stays a quality on every roster file: a commander-type recruit takes the player's orders badly, but can run the crew on a job the player cannot get into. *Direction, not yet a mechanic:* the creator wants the game to become one of real users with their own avatars, each choosing to be a commander or a soldier and gaining power and experience over a long play — not a ten-minute game.
- **15 September 2026 — Three things the game must have.** A pause/menu button (three lines in the top corner, or the Esc key); settings behind it; and an on/off switch for sound. The settings menu is designed as a crime boss's office, from the creator's drawing: a radio with the sound switch and volume knob, a language paper, a display monitor, a controls book, the exit door, and a hat on the hook.
- **15 September 2026 — Music, a tutorial, and an i button on every screen.** The creator's track *"A Name Worth Keeping"* (8:00) plays in the background, looped, switched from the radio in the office. A tutorial walks a new player through the screens on the first game and can be run again. An **i** button sits beside the menu button on every screen and explains whatever is on screen.
- **15 September 2026 — No "dark web".** The term is retired; jobs are posted *online*. Applied everywhere in the game, the README and this summary. The original description above is kept as it was written.
- **15 September 2026 — A live job report, a ranking that rises and falls, and a crew that grows.** Every job plays out minute by minute in text before the verdict — who got in, who talked, what each technique bought, what went right and wrong, the exit, the split. The crew's **ranking** replaces "notoriety": it rises with clean and successful jobs according to the job and what the crew did, and falls with messy, botched or disastrous work, a leak, an arrest or a betrayal — itemised on every end-of-job screen. Everyone who goes on a job gains experience; ranks are earned, and good work adds to the attributes the job leaned on. The saved game is offered first on the title screen so a player continues where they left off.
- **15 September 2026 — The Jobs board: postings on the left, the world map on the right.** The "Online" tab is renamed **Jobs**. A schematic world map (continents in ink, no borders) lights every country that has a posting; choosing a job zooms the map to that country, centred and spotlit with the rest dimmed, while the job file opens in the left column beside it. Clicking a lit country shows only its postings.
- **15 September 2026 — The soundtrack, the report's pace, the case file, and ticks on the job file.** The creator's 14-track compilation replaces the earlier background track: eleven tracks rotate under the live job report, three score the verdict (the quietest for a success, a middle one for a messy job, the loudest for a disaster), and the radio is quiet between jobs. The live report reads at a slower default with five speeds in settings and on the report itself. Every job stays on file: the case log's week is a dropdown to any past week, and each job's full report can be reopened. On the job file every requirement — crew size, techniques, knowledge, language, the job's conditions — carries a green tick or a red cross, with the chip's border coloured to match.
- **15 September 2026 — The report speaks to the player as "you".** The live job report never names the player in the third person: it is "You do the talking", "You (Launderer) have an account waiting", "You can't get in". Its vocabulary is widened — several beats per technique, five turns per job type for good, hitch and blow, several arrivals, language lines, exits, mornings and splits, small texture lines — and a memory of the last sixty lines used keeps a job from repeating a recent one.
- **15 September 2026 — A cause, a consequence and a result for everything.** The creator's rule: *every single thing in this game should have a meaning and a purpose, a cause, a consequence and a result.* Three systems follow from it. **The twist:** on a random share of jobs (one good fit in four, one long shot in twelve) the live report stops mid-way — the van won't start, the inside man wants double, the plan is last year's, a checkpoint, a police radio, a crew member hit, the alarm early, a grey car — and six options wait for the player's call. Only one sets the crew free: the one the crew actually brought can do. The wrong call costs blood, a seat, heat and ranking; walking away keeps everyone and loses the score. **Between jobs:** every few weeks, in rotation, something that is not a job — a death on holiday, a disappearance, a resignation, a defection to another crew that then poaches, an old face who walked with the money, a detective — each a decision with a price. **Ranking with teeth:** the name decides what clients post (tier caps), what they pay (−20% to +35%), who will sign (Veterans need a Known crew, Legends a Respected one), whether loyalty drifts up or down each week, and how hard the police look; a week laid low costs a point. "85, Feared" now means something a client, a recruit and a police force each do differently.
- **15 September 2026 — Seats, other crews, a bonus that buys something, small money, and the loop.** From the creator: *"what's the bonus gives a soldier? other than taking money out of the player's balance?"* — so a bonus now buys three weeks paid up (sharper on the job, deaf to other crews, never the one who skims, not the one trouble picks on) on top of loyalty. *"When you reach a certain level you can expand your crew into 6 soldiers"* — a Respected name (ranking 60) opens seats six and seven. *"When you reach 2 years (104 weeks) and a certain tier with a 7 members crew you can create another crew with a commander and 4 soldiers. and again in 3 years and 4 years. but no more."* — from week 104, Feared, with seven on the crew, a second crew is founded out of the player's own people, named after its commander, taking its own job each week without the player; a third at week 156, a fourth at 208, and no more. *"If the player takes too many low paying jobs the soldiers are starting to get upset from their low cuts"* — a cut under two weeks of a member's own upkeep is small money; three in a row and they grumble, five and they may walk. The creator's hour-long suspense track loops under every screen except the live job report and the verdict, which keep their own tracks.
- **15 September 2026 — Milestones are announced.** From the creator: *"when a player's reaching one and second tiers of expanding a crew and founding a second crew and third, etc., it should be announced by a pop up box in the center of the screen."* Each milestone is announced once, in a box in the middle of the screen: the two seats a Respected name opens; each year mark when another crew is within reach, listing what is still missing; and the moment a second, third or fourth crew can be founded, with a button straight into founding it.
- **15 September 2026 — A tutorial the first time, a reminder the second.** From the creator: *"the game needs a tutorial every first time someone is playing the game, and a reminder tutorial every second time they play."* The first sitting opens with the full tutorial, rewritten to cover the game as it now is (seats, other crews, the money rules, twists, what happens between jobs, milestones). The second sitting — a return after half an hour or more away — opens with a five-card reminder. Neither runs again on its own; both stay one click away — and, from the creator: *"also add a free play tutorial in the settings whenever the player want to recap"* — a tutorial clipboard hangs on the office wall: the whole thing, the reminder, or any one card, any time.
- **15 September 2026 — Female faces.** The women's side of the portrait drawer had 432 distinct looks against 1,080 for the men. Six hair styles (braid, bun, afro, headscarf, wavy, crop) and a third brow set bring it to 1,080, the same as the men. Men's faces are unchanged; women on an existing roster get their new faces once, on the next load.
- **15 September 2026 — Six things for the average player.** Asked what the game was still missing, and told to build all of it. **Who goes:** every job took the whole crew, so picking the team was not a decision; now anyone eligible can be benched on the job file — no cut, no experience, no way to get hurt, and a smaller crew in the field. **Money with somewhere to go:** the balance only ever climbed; now a lawyer, a doctor and a fixer sit on weekly retainers paid out of the same payroll as the crew, a safe house is bought outright in three moves, and a desk can be paid to lose a file. **A goal in the middle distance:** the record counts everything the crew has done, and eighteen career marks run from the first job to the final score, each paying ranking or money. **An antagonist with a face:** a rival outfit with a named boss works the same board and takes the best posting left most weeks, and a named detective keeps a file that brings the raid forward as it thickens. **People, not stats:** two files in three carry a temperament trait, and everyone who works together builds a bond from −3 to +3 that the reckoning counts. **Save portability:** the office file cabinet writes the game out as one line of text and reads it back in, on any browser.
- **15 September 2026 — A face built part by part, and a temperament for the player.** The portrait drawer became a set of explicit choices — hair or hat, face shape, eyebrows, eyes, nose, mouth, ears, glasses, facial hair or earrings, a scar — one slider each on the dossier screen, laid out under the picture and to the left of "Your file", from the creator's mock-up. The standalone "New likeness" button is gone; a small Random rolls every part at once. Three million faces for a man, four and a half million for a woman. Roster files still roll their parts from their own seed. On temperament: hired crew already carried their traits through an old save (the roster rebuilds from the seed and an absent override leaves it alone) — the one file with none was the player's own, which now gets a temperament and a face derived from its own seed the first time an old file is opened.
- **15 September 2026 — What the job actually is.** From the creator: *"there should be a short brief or job description about what the job is about in the job listing once you click it."* The job file opened with the country and never said what the work was. It now opens with four sentences built from the posting: the work in the words of its own category, how well the thing is held (from the tier), the one condition that matters (from the tags), what the client asked for (from the techniques and knowledge), and the terms (from the client and the fee). Generated from a hash of the posting's own id, title and country, so it never changes between openings or across a save, and stores nothing. The final score gets its own words instead.
- **15 September 2026 — A week of reading is worth something, if the job comes off.** From the creator, on the line that read *"Nobody here is a Enforcer; Adaeze watches a video about it."*: *"it needs to be more like 'Nobody here is an Enforcer; Adaeze was learning the trade before the operation' and if she did and the job was successful the soldier that learned a trade for the job should get extra BRN but not high. like +1 and it needs to be mentioned in the 'what they learned' space."* So a missing trade is no longer a shrug. Somebody on the crew spends the week before learning enough of it to stand in, the report names them, and if the verdict is Success or Clean that week is worth **one point of brains** — marked on the end-of-job screen as *stood in as a Safecracker*, beside the point it bought. A job that doesn't come off teaches them nothing. Two gaps are two people's weeks; a crew smaller than the number of gaps means somebody covers two and still learns the one thing. Two grammar bugs went with it: the article now comes from `anA()`, so it is "an Enforcer" and "now an Operator", never "a Enforcer"; and two points of the same attribute read "+2 BRN" instead of "+1 BRN, +1 BRN".
- **15 September 2026 — The long game.** From the creator, at the end of a run that finished in thirty weeks: *"the game is too short please check all the game physics and systems and make it harder and much longer. this should be a few months play at best. and also it should be eventually an online game.....keep that in mind."*

  **What was actually wrong.** Measured before anything was changed, with a headless simulator playing the game two hundred times: a competent crew finished in a **median of eighteen weeks and ten jobs, and nine of every ten jobs came back CLEAN**. The cause was one number. Crew effectiveness runs past 150; difficulty was *capped at 96*. Once the crew was any good, no posting on the board could refuse it, and the only ending — one job that appeared at ranking 100 and needed one Success — arrived before the crew had a second technique between them.

  **What the physics do now.** The cap is gone: the world answers the name. Difficulty carries a pressure term that rises with ranking (+62 at the top of the ladder) and a local term for a country worked lately, which burns and cools a point a week, so the map is something to rotate around. The verdict bands widened and the roll went from ±18 to ±26, so CLEAN is something you prepare for rather than the default: **31% clean, 36% success, 23% messy, 11% blown**. The ladder went from six names across a hundred points to **eight across six hundred**, gains damp as the name grows, and a job well beneath your name barely moves it — grinding the easiest posting was the fastest and dullest way up, and is now neither. Penalties are scaled the same way as rewards: the first attempt scaled only the rewards, which made small work net-negative and pinned every run at ranking 160 however well it was played.

  **What makes it long.** A job is **weeks, not a week** — one for an errand, three for a vault, four for the last score — which is also what makes a second crew earning without you worth founding. Before taking a posting you can **case** it: a week and a fee for +9 on the reckoning, up to four weeks, and the risk that the client stops waiting while you sit outside in a car. Crews get better **slowly** — an attribute at 85 barely moves, Legend is 900 xp — so a crew of Legends is a career rather than ten jobs. And the ending is **three operations in order** against the Committee, gated on a name at the top of the board, fifty-five jobs, five in the field and a five-million war chest; a stage that goes wrong costs weeks, not the game.

  **The economy had to follow the clock.** Three couplings only showed up in simulation. A three-week job paying a one-week fee starved everyone, so pay scales with weeks on the ground. Six Legends at 13–18% each took ninety per cent of every score, so building the crew the game wants you to build bankrupted you — cuts were halved. And halving the cuts made almost every job read as an insult to the crew, because "small money" was measured against two weeks' upkeep; that test had to move with them.

  **Result**, on the same simulator: a median of **348 weeks and 137 jobs**, 92% of runs won, 8% lost, with thirty people hurt, arrested or killed along the way. Roughly twenty times the game. Said plainly: that is six to twelve hours, not a few months — weeks are not hours, and the honest way to months is real-time pacing, not bigger numbers.

  **Kept in mind for online.** Nothing here assumes a single player. The weekly tick is already the turn boundary a server would run on; rival outfits already work the same board and take the best posting left, which is exactly the slot a real player fills; country heat is shared-world state that a crew burns for everyone; all state is in one serializable object with a seeded, deterministic RNG, so a server can re-derive a run and check it. No wall-clock dependency was added to any game rule.

  Every number that decides the curve now lives in one `BAL` object at the top of the script, and `tools/sim.js` is kept in the repo so the next change to it can be measured rather than guessed at.
- **15 September 2026 — Places, a recruitment trip, and what to do about somebody who has left.** Four things from the creator, on a build-24 screen.

  **"Does a seat is the proper term?"** No — it is boardroom English for a crime crew. The word is now **place**, everywhere it means a slot on a crew, and nowhere it means a seat in a car or on a plane, which is a different word doing its own job. "Place 2 of 5 — empty", "seven places", "no place free", "Every place is taken". One sweep, sixty-odd strings, and a test that fails if *seat* comes back to the crew screen.

  **"The Second Crew should say -lock as well like seats 6-7."** It does, and it is drawn the same way — faded, dashed, hatched — so *locked* reads identically wherever it appears. While fixing it: the locked card said "A Respected name (ranking 60)" three builds after the ladder moved Respected to 65, so the threshold is now read off `RANKS` instead of written into the copy.

  **"Recruiting a new crew member should open a live feed window just like a job… the commander flying the potential operator country… randomly high % of them will provide difficulties in the recruitment process especially those with low ranking in important criteria… don't make it a payment thing only. Make it as an actual physical recruitment process for each one every single time."** So hiring is no longer a button and a fee. **Go and ask** flies you to wherever they live and puts it to them in person: **a week off the calendar**, a live feed on the same ticker as a job — the border, the money changed badly twice, the bar with two doors, how they read you across the table — and their answer at the end of it. The **fee is only paid if they sign**; turn back or get turned down and the money stays with you while the week does not.

  How hard a file is to recruit is a number, shown on the last screen, and **both ends of the roster are difficult for opposite reasons**: greed, years inside, a temperament, a hard border and **low numbers in the things that matter** all push it up, because desperate people make a mess of a meeting — while a **Legend** is difficult because they test you. A name at the top of the board makes every meeting easier.

  Eight ways a meeting goes wrong, and **none of them is solved by paying**: they don't show, the number moves, they bring somebody who is not introduced, a car sits outside for forty minutes, they ask what happened to the last person in the job, three men and a shut door, they have been drinking since lunch, two officers in the lobby at seven. What answers it is **what you brought** — an enforcer in the room, a face to do the talking, a forger for papers, a fixer to find out who moved them, the local language, your own Charm or Nerve, or a record long enough to answer with. Walking out is always on the list. A refusal costs nothing but the week, and they will not meet again for a couple of months.

  **"If a crew member leaves the question of should we waste him needs to be asked. And if so - how and the risks of getting heat from that and the % of it."** Every way out of the crew that leaves somebody breathing — walking out over the money, taking a cut of the score and vanishing, going to a rival, resigning, being cut loose — now puts the question in the middle of the screen, and it cannot be clicked away. It says how many jobs they worked with you and **the percentage they eventually talk**, because a talker is not an event: it is a slow leak, putting something else in the detective's file every few weeks for as long as they are alive.

  **Every answer carries its own number.** Let them go, and the percentage stands. Buy the silence, and it drops — and will be asked for again. A word from your enforcer costs heat and some of the crew's good opinion. Your fixer can put them four borders away. And the rest end it: a **cleaner** makes it an accident, 86% clean and the most expensive; a stranger you never meet, 62%; your enforcer tonight, cheapest and loudest at 50%, and when it goes wrong it is your enforcer the police take. Each says what happens when it does not go cleanly — heat, the detective's file, an arrest.

  It works, and that is the point: a body does not talk, and the board notices what happens to people who leave your crew — **ranking +2** when it is clean. It also costs **loyalty across everybody still with you**, because they can all count, and nobody is safe working for somebody who does that. Doing it badly costs three points of ranking and a detective with a body to put next to your alias.

  **Two real bugs came out of testing this.** *Skip* on a live feed checked only for a job's twist, so it jumped a recruitment trip straight past its meeting and left the trip unresolved and the week unspent. And a refusal still charged the full fee, because the fee and the incidentals — a second hotel, a bribe, a bar tab, an envelope taken off you in a back room — were one number; they are two now, and only the incidentals are paid when nobody signs.

  **What it does to the game.** On the same simulator, with it driving trips and answering the question: a median run is **338 weeks and 90 jobs**, with 37 trips of which a dozen go wrong, a couple of refusals, and a few people dealt with. Runs that end in losing went from 8% to **37%** — losing somebody now costs a week to replace and the replacement can say no, so a crew wipe is much harder to come back from. That is the direction that was asked for.
- **15 September 2026 — Trade, a brief where the word is first met, and a face you can see while you choose it.** Three notes from the creator on the dossier screen.

  **"Is it the best way to call it? Technique??"** No. The job report had already been calling them **trades** for three builds — *"stood in as a Safecracker"*, *"nobody here is an Enforcer; she was learning the trade before the operation"*, *"a trade the crew hasn't got"* — so the game was using two words for one thing and teaching the wrong one first. It is **trade** everywhere now: on the dossier, the job files, the roster filter, the crew cards, the hover briefs and the tutorial. Thirty strings, one word, and the report no longer contradicts the label.

  **"When you choose your trade in the 'who are you?' screen, there should be that short brief of the trades as well. That's the first time the player is introduced to those techniques."** Right — sixteen job titles with nothing under them, and the only explanation was a hover brief on a screen the player has not reached yet. The dossier now prints the trade's own line under the dropdown, and follows the dropdown: what it is (*"Gets everyone out before the sirens"*), what it leans on (*Nerve*), and what it comes with (*Logistics*). The lines already existed on `TECHS`; they were simply never shown at the one moment somebody needs them.

  **"How can I choose a face feature if when I'm scrolling down to the next feature, the face is no longer in the viewport?"** A fair description of a broken screen: ten sliders is taller than a laptop window, so choosing a nose meant choosing it blind. The picture is now **sticky** — it pins to the top of its column and stays there while the parts scroll past it, at every one of the ten and at phone width. The rows were also shortened from three lines to two, the part's name and its current choice sharing a line above their own slider, which takes about 140px out of the column. A test drives all ten parts on a 1280×760 window and fails if the picture is not on screen at any of them.

  One thing that came out of it: with the value auto-placed it landed *below* its own slider and read as the next part's, so the rows are placed explicitly now.
- **15 September 2026 — A client who takes it personally.** From the creator: *"When a percentage of works is Botched. randomly, an unsatisfied client will be revengeful and will either contact other clients and you'll see you crew is banned for certain jobs during the game or he will try to revenge you and your crew by attacking or robbing you guys."*

  Until now a botched job cost money, ranking, heat and sometimes a person, and the client it was botched for simply posted again the following week as though nothing had happened. Now they remember, and the two outcomes are the two the creator named.

  **The word goes round** — the common one. About a third of botched jobs and two in three disasters offend the client, more on a big fee. That client stops posting for twenty to thirty weeks, has a word with one or two of the people they drink with (who stop for eight to twenty), and **the postings they already had come off the board immediately**. The Jobs board carries a line naming who will not deal with you and until when, because a board with fewer jobs on it and no explanation is a bug as far as a player is concerned.

  **Or they come and find you** — about two in five of the grudges, and more after a disaster. Two to six weeks later, one of four scenes: three cars outside the safe house at four in the morning; the count short because somebody walked into the flat in daylight; a detective outside the wrong café two days running; or somebody buying drinks for the least loyal member of the crew. Which one you get depends on who you have. **Every answer carries the odds it goes your way**, printed on it, and what is open depends on what you brought — an enforcer to meet them at the door or collect what was taken, a fixer to find out who carried it or what was said, a safe house to walk away from, or the cash to pay back what they lost. The board warns you it is coming: *and one of them is not finished with you*.

  **Paying is the one certain answer, and only one kind of paying counts.** Giving the client back what they lost, in cash, at the door, always ends it and is the only thing that lifts the ban as well. Paying a police desk settles a file; paying your own man a bonus settles your own man. The first draft had all three of them clearing the client's grudge, which is not how a grudge works — a browser drive caught it by paying a police desk and finding the client had forgiven you.

  **Three other things the tests caught.** A robbery took a percentage of the whole float, so a rich crew was robbed of eleven million over a job that paid two hundred thousand; it is now bounded by roughly what the client lost. Client names carry their own article — *a shipping line*, *an heir*, *the Notary* — so capitalising them mid-sentence produced "A shipping line did not come for you; A shipping line made a phone call"; the templates now take `{C}` at the start of a sentence and `{c}` inside one. And a job title is a verb phrase, so "a phone call about Open the port authority network" now reads "about the port authority network", using the same `jobNoun()` the brief already uses.

  On the simulator, driving all of it: a median run sees about **one client at the door** and a couple of quieter blacklistings, which is the right ratio — the board shrinking is the common consequence and somebody at the door is the rare one. Median length, verdict mix and win rate are unchanged: this is a cost on the jobs that already went wrong, not a new tax on the ones that go right.
- **15 September 2026 — The tabs pin too.** From the creator: *"I would like the top bar (crew | Jobs | Roster | Log) to be pinned at all time so when you scroll down it always available like the top The Crew bar."* The top bar was already sticky; the tab row was not, so on a long roster or a full board every tab was a scroll away. It now pins directly under the top bar. The offset is the top bar's **measured** height rather than a number, because that bar wraps to two rows on a phone — 46px on a laptop, 123px at 400px wide — and a hardcoded value would leave a gap at one width and hide the first row of tabs at the other. Measured on every render and on every resize. A drive scrolls 2,500px down the roster and fails if the two bars are not flush, at both widths, and clicks a tab from the bottom of the page to prove it still works.
- **15 September 2026 — No phone version, said out loud.** From the creator, on being shown the tabs pinned at 400px wide: *"No phone version is needed at all."*

  Worth checking before cutting anything, and the check was the interesting part: **there was never a phone version to cut.** There is no `<meta name="viewport">` in the file, which means a real phone lays the page out at its default ~980px and zooms out — so the `max-width:520/640/700/720/900` rules almost never fire on a phone at all. They fire in a **resized desktop window**.
