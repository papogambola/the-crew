What it is

You are the commander. You start with a name, a passport and four empty places at the table.

Six thousand people are looking for work. Every one of them has a trade, a history, a country that will or will not have them, and a price. Some are lying about all four. You read the files, you make offers, and you find out which was which later, usually at the worst possible hour.

Jobs come in from clients around the world — a recovery in Piraeus, an escort over a Polish pass, something in Osaka that nobody will put in writing. Each wants particular trades, particular languages and a particular amount of nerve. Each one expires. Spend three weeks thinking about it and a rival crew takes it while you think.

Then you send them, and it runs hour by hour, and something goes wrong. Something always goes wrong. What you do about it is the whole game: six ways out, none of them right, and the crew you spent a year building is the only thing standing between the plan and the police.

Money, heat and a ranking that other crews are climbing too. Nothing resets at the end of a week. People you cut loose remember that you cut them loose.

Black ink, white paper and a great deal of nerve. No account to make, one thing to buy inside it — the door, $12, once — and no tutorial you can’t skip. It runs on your machine and saves on your machine.

What it needs

System
Windows, macOS or Linux. It is one page of HTML with nothing underneath it, so the machine hardly matters.
Browser
Any current one — Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari.
Screen
1280 × 800 or wider. It is a game for a desk; there is no phone layout and there isn’t going to be one.
Graphics
Nothing in particular. It is type and line art. It will run on a laptop from 2014.
Installing
Nothing to install. No download, no unzipping, no security warning to click past, and nothing left on your disk that you have to find again later.
Internet
To load the page, and to stream the music. The game itself is one file — once it is open, everything it does it does on your machine.
Price
Twelve weeks of play free, or until the name reaches Known. Then $12, once, for lifetime access — no subscription, no account, a key on the receipt that opens the game anywhere.

Where your game lives

Saving
It saves itself as you play and picks up where you left off. There is no save button and nothing to name. Close the tab whenever you like.
Where
In this browser, filed under this site — not on a server, and not anywhere we can see. Nobody has your crew but you.
What loses it
Clearing your browsing data, or a private window, which keeps nothing by design. A different browser or a different computer is a different drawer, so the game will not be there either.
Carrying it
The filing cabinet in the office (Esc, under the desk on the right) writes the whole game out as a line of text. Keep it anywhere — an email to yourself will do — and paste it back in on any machine to carry the crew across. That is also the only real backup there is, so take one after a night you would hate to repeat.
