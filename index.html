The Crew

You are the commander. You start with a name, a passport and four empty places at the table.

People are looking for work, and every one of them has a trade, a history, a country that will or will not have them, and a price. Some are lying about all four. You read the files, you make offers, and you find out which was which later, usually at the worst possible hour.

Jobs come in from clients around the world — a recovery in Piraeus, an escort over a Polish pass, something in Osaka that nobody will put in writing. Each wants particular trades, particular languages and a particular amount of nerve. Each one expires. Spend three weeks thinking about it and a rival crew takes it while you think.

Then you send them, and it runs hour by hour, and something goes wrong. Something always goes wrong. What you do about it is the whole game: six ways out, none of them right, and the crew you spent a year building is the only thing standing between the plan and the police.

Money, heat and a ranking that other crews are climbing too. Nothing resets at the end of a week. People you cut loose remember that you cut them loose.

Black ink, white paper and a great deal of nerve, and no tutorial you can’t skip.

One week, and how it ends

Seven jobs on the board.
Five people with records.
One brief worth the heat — and a harbour café at 11:32, where it stops going to plan.

What it needs

System
Windows, macOS or Linux. It is one page of HTML with nothing underneath it, so the machine hardly matters.
Browser
Any current one — Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari.
Screen
1280 × 800 or wider. It is a game for a desk; there is no phone layout and there isn’t going to be one.
Graphics
Nothing in particular. It is type and line art. It will run on a laptop from 2014.
Installing
Nothing to install. No download, no unzipping, no security warning to click past, and nothing left on your disk that you have to find again later.
Internet
To load the page, and to stream the music. The game itself is one file — once it is open, everything it does it does on your machine.
Price
$12, once, paid before you play, taken by Stripe — which shows you whichever ways to pay it can offer where you are. No subscription, no second purchase, and no key to keep: it is your account that is paid for, so signing in is what opens the game, anywhere.

Where your game lives

Saving
It saves itself as you play and picks up where you left off. There is no save button and nothing to name. Close the tab whenever you like.
Where
In this browser first, and mirrored to your account so it is not only here. Nobody reads it but you — it goes up as one opaque blob.
What loses it
Nothing, while you are signed in — that is what the account is for. Clearing your browsing data empties this browser's copy, and the one on the account is still there to fetch back. A private window keeps nothing by design, and cannot hold a sign-in either, so the game will not open in one.
Carrying it
Sign in on the other machine; the dossiers are on the account and you fetch the one you want. The filing cabinet in the office (Esc, under the desk on the right) also writes the whole game out as a line of text — keep it anywhere, an email to yourself will do — which is the backup that depends on nothing but you.

Anything wrong with it

Bugs, a crew member who did something inexplicable, a screen that made no sense — say so, and say which build you were on (it is printed at the bottom of every screen in the game).
